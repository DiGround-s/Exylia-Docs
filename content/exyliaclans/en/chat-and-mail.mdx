## Channels

```
/clan chat
```

Cycles the player's channel. There are three, in order:

| Channel | Reaches | Format key |
|---|---|---|
| `GLOBAL` | Normal server chat | — |
| `CLAN` | The clan | `clan.chat-format` |
| `CLAN_AND_ALLIES` | The clan and every allied clan | `clan.ally-chat-format` |

While the channel is anything but `GLOBAL`, everything the player types is captured and sent there
instead. The channel is per session — it resets to `GLOBAL` on logout, and also resets itself if the
player stops being in a clan.

```yaml
clan:
  chat-format: "{success}[CLAN] {muted}%player%{muted}: {letters}%message%"
  ally-chat-format: "{info}[ALLY] {muted}%player%{muted}: {letters}%message%"
```

`/clan chat <message>` sends one line to the current channel without switching to it.

## Quick prefixes

```yaml
settings:
  chat:
    quick-clan-prefixes: ["."]
    quick-ally-prefixes: ["@"]
```

Typing `.on my way` in normal chat sends *on my way* to the clan; `@push now` sends *push now* to the
clan and its allies. The channel is not changed — the next line goes wherever it was going before.

Both lists take as many prefixes as you like, and either can be emptied to disable that shortcut.

<Callout type="warning" title="A prefix swallows the start of a normal message">
`.` is a common way to start a sentence. Anybody who types `...` or `.gg` is talking to their clan
without meaning to. Two- or three-character prefixes (`c:`, `!!`) are far less surprising; picking them
is a one-line change.
</Callout>

Messages are escaped before they are formatted: MiniMessage tags and `%` are neutralised, so a player
cannot inject a colour, a click event or a placeholder into what their clan reads.

## Mail

```
/clan mail                              open the inbox
/clan mail send <member> <message>      one member
/clan mail sendclan <message>           the whole clan
```

Mail is stored, so it is what clan chat is not: a message that is still there when the recipient logs in
tomorrow. Sending to the whole clan needs `SEND_CLAN_MAIL`; sending to one member needs nothing beyond
being in the same clan.

```yaml
mail:
  expiry-days: 14
  expiry-check-interval-minutes: 30
  max-message-length: 256
  max-mails-per-clan: 100
```

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| `expiry-days` | Days before a mail is deleted. `0` never expires. |
| `expiry-check-interval-minutes` | How often expired mail is purged. |
| `max-message-length` | Longest body, in characters. |
| `max-mails-per-clan` | How many a clan keeps. The oldest **unpinned** are pruned past this. `0` disables. |

### Read, deleted and pinned

Read and deleted are tracked **per member**: the same clan mail can be unread for one player, read by
another and deleted by a third, all from one row. Deleting is therefore only ever your own copy — the
mail stays in everybody else's inbox.

Pinning is clan-wide. A pinned mail sits at the top of the inbox and is exempt from the
`max-mails-per-clan` prune, which is what makes it the right place for the rules of a raid or a schedule.

The inbox menu also offers **mark all read**. On join, a player with unread mail is told how many they
have, and `%exyliaclans_clan_mail_unread%` is the number for a scoreboard.

<Callout type="note" title="Composing goes through chat, not an anvil">
Sending from the menu asks for the subject and then the body as chat input, using the prompts in
`mail.subject-prompt` and `mail.message-prompt`. The command form skips the subject and uses
`mail.default-subject`.
</Callout>

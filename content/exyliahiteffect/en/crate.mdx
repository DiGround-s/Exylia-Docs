---
title: "The crate"
description: "Keys, rarities and the reels: how an effect is won instead of granted."
---

An effect can be given by a permission node, or it can be won. The crate is the second way: a player
spends keys, watches reels fall, and keeps whatever they land on — written to their own row rather
than to a rank, so it outlives the rank that paid for it.

```yaml title="config.yml"
crate:
  enabled: true
  start-keys: 0
  duplicate-refund: 1
  reward: UNLOCK
  blocks: []
  max-at-once: 4
  spin-frames: 34
  stagger-seconds: 2.0
```

## Keys are not an item

A key lives on the player's row, next to their chosen effect and their shortlist. Nothing can drop
one, dupe one, or lose one to a full inventory, and nothing picks one up off the floor.

The only way in is the admin command, which a shop, a vote listener or a reward plugin runs as
console:

```text
/hiteffectsadmin keys give <player> <amount>
/hiteffectsadmin keys take <player> <amount>
/hiteffectsadmin keys set <player> <amount>
/hiteffectsadmin keys check <player>
```

`start-keys` is what a player is handed the first time the plugin sees them — a way to let everybody
open one before they are asked to buy the next.

## Rarities

Every effect belongs to a rarity, written as `tier:` in `effects.yml`. The rarity decides three
things: the colour the effect is named in, how often a reel stops on it, and what
`exyliahiteffect.tier.<id>` grants.

```yaml title="config.yml"
tiers:
  common:
    name: "Common"
    material: GRAY_DYE
    color: "{muted}"
    chance: 60.0
    priority: 1
  legendary:
    name: "Legendary"
    material: ORANGE_DYE
    color: "{highlight}"
    chance: 3.0
    priority: 4
```

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `name` | What menus and placeholders call the rarity. |
| `material` | The item the rarity is drawn with. |
| `color` | The colour its effects are written in. |
| `chance` | How often a reel stops on it, relative to the others. |
| `priority` | Where it sits in menus; lower comes first. |

<Callout type="note" title="chance is a weight, not a percentage">
The four shipped weights happen to add up to 100, which is only a convenience. Delete `epic` and its
twelve are re-spread across the three that are left, rather than becoming a twelve-in-a-hundred hole
where nothing comes out.
</Callout>

Nothing here is hard-coded. Rename `rare`, invent `mythic`, delete `legendary` — an effect naming a
rarity that no longer exists falls back to the first one, so a deleted rarity costs its effects their
colour and never their existence. An effect with no `tier:` at all is common.

## Opening

`/hiteffect crate` — or the button in the top-right corner of the effect menu, or a bound block —
opens `menus/crate.yml`, which asks one question: how many at once. One key opens one crate, and
`max-at-once` caps the answer at seven, which is what the opening screen fits.

Picking four spends four keys and opens four reels side by side on `menus/crate_open.yml`. Each reel
falls down its own column, and each stops `stagger-seconds` after the one before it, so they land one
at a time with a pause to look at each. `spin-frames` is how many faces the first reel runs through
before it stops — higher is a longer fall, capped at 200, which is about eleven seconds.

<Callout type="note" title="Closing mid-fall costs nothing">
The keys are spent and the prizes decided before the first frame is drawn. What a player won is
already theirs and is said in chat, so a disconnect halfway down loses them nothing.
</Callout>

What a reel came within one slot of stays on screen when it stops. Half of what a crate is worth is
the legendary that went past the line, and a column that wipes itself never lets anybody see it.

## Duplicates

A reel that stops on something the player already owns hands back `duplicate-refund` keys instead.
Set it to `0` and a duplicate is worth nothing; set it to `1` and a duplicate is a free retry.

## What a win hands over

`reward` decides what a crate pays out. They are two different economies:

| `reward` | What the player gets |
|---|---|
| `UNLOCK` | The effect is unlocked on their account and picked from `/hiteffect`. It cannot be traded or lost. |
| `ITEM` | Only the token, to be bound to a weapon, sold or given away. Nothing is unlocked. |
| `BOTH` | Unlocked on the account, and handed over as a token as well. |

`ITEM` and `BOTH` want `behaviour.mode` set to `weapon` or `both` — a token has nowhere to go
otherwise.

<Callout type="note" title="`ITEM` has no duplicates">
Nothing is unlocked under `ITEM`, so a reel can never stop on something already owned. Every opening
is a win, every key pays out, and `duplicate-refund` never applies.
</Callout>

## Crate blocks

A block in the world can open the crate. Look at it and run:

```text
/hiteffectsadmin crate block add
/hiteffectsadmin crate block remove
/hiteffectsadmin crate block list
/hiteffectsadmin crate block clear
```

The command writes the block you are looking at into `crate.blocks`, as
`server,world,x,y,z,yaw,pitch`. Bind them in game rather than by hand: a bound block cannot be broken,
blown up or pushed away while it is on the list.

## What a win is worth

An unlocked effect is written to the player's row, not granted as a permission. That is the whole
point of the crate: a rank running out takes its category node with it, and takes nothing that was
won.

Both halves are read together — a player owns an effect if a permission grants it **or** their row
says they unlocked it. Admins can write that half by hand:

```text
/hiteffectsadmin unlocks grant <player> <effect>
/hiteffectsadmin unlocks revoke <player> <effect>
/hiteffectsadmin unlocks clear <player>
```

<Callout type="tip" title="Turning the crate off">
`enabled: false` leaves the button in place and has it say the crate is closed, rather than removing a
thing players had learned to look for.
</Callout>

Writing a hit effect by hand means knowing twenty-six shapes, forty parameters and why the shipped
ones are never longer than six tenths of a second. You do not have to. The prompt below carries all
of it — the step language, the budget, the three rules that save an afternoon, and three shipped
effects in full — so the only thing left for you is the idea.

## Write the idea first

The prompt is only as good as what you put in it. "Something cool" produces something generic; a
paragraph produces the effect you pictured. Say:

<Steps>
  <Step title="What it is made of">
    Real blocks and items, named. `GOLD_BLOCK`, `COBBLESTONE`, `IRON_SWORD`. This is what gives an
    effect its silhouette — particles are only the light around it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One gesture, not a scene">
    A hit effect is a single movement: splinters thrown outwards, a ring that flattens, one blade
    passing through. Say what that movement is and which way it goes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="That it plays on every blow">
    This is the part that matters. Two to five steps, under six tenths of a second, one sound. Ask
    for anything longer and it reads as lag in a real fight, not as an effect.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The mood and the sound">
    Sharp and metallic, dull and heavy, wet, electric. Name a sound if you have one in mind;
    otherwise let the model choose and change it afterwards.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="A worked example of an idea">
"Glass. On the hit, six `GLASS_PANE` shards burst outwards from the chest, small, tumbling, shrinking
to nothing in a third of a second. One flat `WHITE_STAINED_GLASS` ring flattens under them. A high,
brittle break sound. Nothing falls to the ground, nothing lingers."
</Callout>

## Copy the prompt

<AiPrompt id="hiteffect" />

Paste it into whichever AI you use, replace `<<< WRITE YOUR IDEA HERE >>>` with what you wrote above,
and send it.

## Then

<Steps>
  <Step title="Paste the answer into effects.yml">
    Open `plugins/ExyliaHitEffect/effects.yml` and paste the block under `hit_effects:`, at the same
    indentation as the effects already there. Make sure its `category` is one that exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reload">
    `/hiteffectsadmin reload`. Every player picking that effect is re-checked against the new
    catalogue, so an effect that stops existing is dropped from whoever had it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the console">
    A step that cannot be read is dropped and printed with the line that caused it. That is your
    proofreader: if nothing appears, every line was understood.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test it in a real fight, not on a dummy">
    One hit looks fine on its own. Twenty hits in five seconds is the test that matters — if the
    screen fills up, cut a step or halve a `life`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="The model can still be wrong">
It writes a material that does not exist, or a parameter a shape does not understand, and the
console says so. It cannot break the plugin — a bad line is dropped, the rest of the effect still
plays — but it can waste an afternoon if you do not read the console. Give the error back to the AI
and it usually fixes it in one turn.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="It is a permission too">
The key you paste becomes `exyliahiteffect.effect.<key>`, so nobody sees the new effect until they
are granted it. See [Permissions](/docs/exyliahiteffect/permissions).
</Callout>

Everything the prompt knows is documented here too, if you would rather write the effect yourself:
[Sequences](/docs/exyliahiteffect/sequences) is the full step language, and
[Effects and categories](/docs/exyliahiteffect/effects) is the file around it.

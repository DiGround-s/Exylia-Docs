A mode is two decisions at once: **the kit the bot carries** and **the combat model it fights
under**. Picking `CRYSTAL_PVP` puts netherite and blast protection on it *and* gives it obsidian,
crystals, anchors and pearls; picking `SWORD` puts it back in diamond *and* takes every consumable
away. That is why `bot.mode` and `bot.difficulty` are the only two keys in `config.yml` anybody has
to understand — the mode decides which techniques exist, and
[difficulty](/docs/exyliapracticebot/difficulty) decides how well they are executed.

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/config.yml"
bot:
  mode: POT_PVP
  difficulty: NORMAL
```

## The seven modes

| Mode | What it is | Its own config block |
|---|---|---|
| `NONE` | Plain vanilla melee. No techniques, no consumables. The enum's zero value and a sandbox setting. | — |
| `SWORD` | Sword and shield, played straight: combos, w-taps, strafes, almost no crits. | — |
| `POT_PVP` | 1.8-flavoured sword combat on modern mechanics: crits, splash potions, golden apples. | `advanced.pot-pvp` |
| `UHC` | Axe and shield: guard breaks, gapples, cobwebs, lava and water. | `advanced.uhc` |
| `CRYSTAL_PVP` | Obsidian, end crystals, respawn anchors, ender pearls, totems, traps. | `advanced.crystal-pvp` |
| `MACE_PVP` | Wind-charge launches into mace smashes. | — |
| `BOXING` | Hits are counted, not dealt. Pure combo practice. | — |

Only three modes have a block of their own. `advanced.attack`, `advanced.movement`,
`advanced.health` and `advanced.defense` apply to every mode alike — see
[configuration](/docs/exyliapracticebot/configuration).

<Callout type="note" title="Older spellings still parse">
`CombatMode.parse` is case-insensitive, trims whitespace and absorbs the names earlier versions
used, so a config or a database row written by an older build still reads correctly:
`POT`/`POTPVP`/`MEDIUM`/`PRO` → `POT_PVP`, `CRYSTAL`/`CRYSTALPVP` → `CRYSTAL_PVP`,
`MACE`/`MACEPVP` → `MACE_PVP`, `VANILLA`/`SWORDPVP`/`SWORD_PVP` → `SWORD`,
`UHCPVP`/`UHC_PVP` → `UHC`, `BOX`/`BOXINGPVP`/`COMBO` → `BOXING`, and `EASY` → `NONE`.
Anything it does not recognise falls back to `NONE`, so a typo in `bot.mode` is a bot with a sword
and no techniques rather than a startup error.
</Callout>

## The kit each mode dresses in

A fresh bot starts on the kit of its mode, so nobody has to know that crystal PvP wants blast
protection and a totem while pot PvP wants Strength II. **After that the gear is the player's:
switching mode changes the techniques, never the kit**. The kit below is written onto the settings
when they are first built — a player's first bot, and again after `/bot reset` — and when a bot is
spawned through the [API](/docs/exyliapracticebot/api). Cycling the mode in the menu after that
changes the techniques and the combat model and leaves the gear exactly as you set it. Armour is
uniform, four pieces of the same tier with the same enchantment; mixing tiers afterwards is a
per-piece choice in the [equipment](/docs/exyliapracticebot/equipment) menu.

| Mode | Armour | Enchant | Main hand | Off hand | Potions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NONE` | Diamond | Protection IV | Sword | Shield | — |
| `SWORD` | Diamond | Protection IV | Sword | Shield | — |
| `POT_PVP` | Diamond | Protection IV | Sword | Shield | Strength II, Speed II |
| `UHC` | Diamond | Protection IV | Axe | Shield | — |
| `CRYSTAL_PVP` | Netherite | Blast Protection IV | Sword | Totem | — |
| `MACE_PVP` | Netherite | Protection IV | Mace | Totem | — |
| `BOXING` | None | — | Sword | Empty | — |

UHC carries no potion buffs on purpose: in UHC what you drink is what you brewed, and the bot brews
nothing. Boxing carries no armour and nothing in the off hand because nobody is taking damage —
armour would be scenery and a shield would be a way to stop the round happening.

## The combat model

Every shipped mode fights under the modern rules: the attack bar exists, damage scales with it, and
a critical hit is a deliberate jump paid for with the sprint. What differs per mode is how much each
of those is worth.

| Mode | Shield timing | Reach factor | Crit chance | Orbit bias | Stays grounded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NONE` | yes | 1.0 | 0.75 | 1.0 | — |
| `SWORD` | yes | 1.0 | 0.12 | 1.7 | — |
| `POT_PVP` | no | 0.92 | 1.0 | 0.8 | — |
| `UHC` | yes | 1.0 | 0.35 | 1.2 | — |
| `CRYSTAL_PVP` | yes | 0.55 | 0.0 | 1.0 | yes |
| `MACE_PVP` | yes | 1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | — |
| `BOXING` | no | 0.95 | 0.0 | 1.5 | — |

Reach factor multiplies the distance the bot likes to hold, so a crystal bot stands roughly half as
far out as a sword bot. Orbit bias multiplies how often it circles instead of walking straight in:
above one is a format decided by movement, below one a format decided by pressure. "Stays grounded"
is crystal PvP only — a blast under your feet hurts far more from above it, so the crit jump and the
jump reset are taken away.

Weapons keep their vanilla cadence: a sword charges in 12.5 ticks, an axe in 20, a mace in 33.3.
`advanced.attack.speed-multiplier` multiplies whichever the mode chose.

## `NONE` — sandbox

The zero value. Same kit as `SWORD`, no style of its own, and a high crit chance — 0.75, second only
to pot PvP — because nothing in it argues against jumping. The API's `CombatMode.playable()` leaves
it out of any list built for a player to choose from, but the in-game menu cycles through all seven,
so it is reachable there. It is mostly what an unparseable name falls back to, and a plain dummy for
a server that wants one.

## `SWORD` — movement

There is no consumable and no gimmick, so there is nothing for a style to own: the crits it hardly
takes and the strafing it lives on belong to the brain, and every bot gets them. Which makes `SWORD`
the honest read on [difficulty](/docs/exyliapracticebot/difficulty) — aim, reaction, spacing,
sprint resets and cooldown discipline with nothing on top.

The crit is deliberately rare here. Nine ticks of stalling and a dropped sprint buy one hit and a
half, and cost the strafe the format is actually won with, so the bot circles more than in any other
mode and jumps in almost none of them.

## `POT_PVP` — the resource game

The techniques that decide a pot fight are already in the brain. What the style owns is the resource
game, and that is what separates a good pot player from a bad one.

- **Splash first, eat second.** Instant Health II lands at the bot's own feet and costs it a tick
  and no ground, so it is the heal the fight is played with. The golden apple is thirty-two ticks of
  standing still for a payout that arrives afterwards, and it is only started from a position that
  survives thirty-two ticks.
- **Heal on your terms.** The apple — and only the apple — is worth backing out of reach for,
  shield up, eaten there. A low-patience bot will start eating in the middle of a combo, which is
  exactly the mistake that loses the round.
- **Punish the heal.** The moment the opponent raises a hand to eat, the bot commits.
- **Double pot.** Below four hearts one splash was never going to be enough, so the second follows
  three or four ticks later rather than after the health bar is read again.

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/config.yml"
advanced:
  pot-pvp:
    apple-percent: 35.0
    potion-percent: 70.0
    enchanted-apples: false
```

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `apple-percent` | `35.0` | Health percentage at which it starts looking for a safe moment to eat. Keep it low: the apple is the heal of last resort, not the one the fight is played with. |
| `potion-percent` | `70.0` | Health percentage at which it splashes Instant Health II immediately. This is the first-line heal and should sit well above `apple-percent`. |
| `enchanted-apples` | `false` | Eat enchanted golden apples instead of normal ones. |

Both percentages are the *centre* of a threshold, not the threshold: each bot rolls its own bias
once and keeps it — up to six points either side for the potion, eight for the apple. Two bots on
the same difficulty that both reach for a potion at exactly the same health are two copies of one
script; two that reach at 64 % and 76 % are two players.

A bot spawned with no kit carries 18 splash potions and 8 golden apples, and restocks after seven
seconds with no hit landed either way — the way somebody walks back to their chest between rounds.

## `UHC` — deciding where the fight happens

UHC is the format with no brewing stand: there is one heal, it takes thirty-two ticks of standing
still, and everything else in the kit is a way of deciding where the fight happens.

- **The guard break is the read.** An axe swing puts a raised shield on cooldown, and the bot does
  it on every swing it lands with an axe in hand. A bot whose main hand was changed in the menu
  still knows the technique: it brings an axe out for one swing and puts it away.
- **The web is a combo, not a trap.** It goes at their feet only inside 4.5 blocks, because a webbed
  opponent one block away is four free hits and a webbed opponent six blocks away is four free
  seconds to eat.
- **The lava is a wall.** Dumped at their feet from 2.6 to 6.5 blocks out, and only against somebody
  already below 55 % health — at full health a burn is four hearts and a reason to eat. It is
  placed without a block update, so the source never flows anywhere, and it is picked back up two
  seconds later.
- **The water is the other half of the lava.** On fire or standing in it, the bot puts itself out
  with a bucket at its own feet rather than burning to death next to its own trap.

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/config.yml"
advanced:
  uhc:
    apple-percent: 60.0
    cobweb: true
    lava: true
    water: true
```

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `apple-percent` | `60.0` | Health percentage at which it eats a golden apple. UHC has no splash potions, so this sits far higher than the pot PvP equivalent. Carries the same per-bot bias of up to eight points either side. |
| `cobweb` | `true` | Trap the opponent in a cobweb to hold them still for a combo. |
| `lava` | `true` | Dump lava at the opponent's feet. |
| `water` | `true` | Carry a water bucket, to put itself out and to wash lava away. |

Everything the bot puts down is taken back up: a cobweb after three seconds, lava after two, water
after one, and all of it immediately when the bot is removed. A bot spawned with no kit carries 6
apples, 4 cobwebs, 2 lava buckets and 2 water buckets, and restocks after seven quiet seconds.

## `CRYSTAL_PVP` — a rehearsed sequence

Crystal PvP is not a chance to place a crystal every tick, it is a rehearsed sequence, so the bot
runs a state machine and the difficulty only decides how fast the machine runs:

```text
IDLE   pick a spot beside the opponent and inside your own reach
PREP   face it, sneak, lay obsidian - a crystal goes on nothing else
ARM    set the crystal down
POP    shield up, swing at it, take your share of the blast
DTAP   second crystal into the knockback, past the invulnerability window
RESET  sword back in hand, breathe
```

What makes it read as a crystal player rather than as a script:

- **It never leaves the floor.** The crit jump and the jump reset — free value everywhere else —
  are how a crystal player kills themselves, so this mode takes them away.
- **It closes, it never kites.** The style never asks to retreat and never stops to eat. The health
  bar is defended with totems and with the opponent being dead.
- **It makes the height it needs.** Swing first; the hit lifts the opponent off the floor for a
  moment; obsidian goes into the block they just vacated; they land on top of it a block higher and
  the next crystal sits below them, which is where a crystal does its damage.
- **Height decides the weapon.** Against somebody on flat ground the obsidian under a crystal is
  between them and the blast, so the bot reaches for the anchor instead: it takes the floor out, and
  the crater it leaves is where the next crystal goes.
- **It answers your crystals.** An armed crystal within reach is somebody else's damage on a timer,
  so the bot raises its shield and sets it off itself before you can.
- **It builds defensively.** Two blasts from underneath in quick succession and it fills the block
  the blast came out of, or puts one block of obsidian on the line between itself and the hole.
- **Its own blast hurts it.** The explosion does not exclude the bot, so placement scoring and the
  self-preservation floor are decisions with a real price.

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/config.yml"
advanced:
  crystal-pvp:
    range: 5.0
    self-preservation-percent: 30.0
    anchor: true
    trap: true
    ender-pearls: true
    break-blocks: true
```

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `range` | `5.0` | Blocks from the opponent within which it will start a crystal chain. |
| `self-preservation-percent` | `30.0` | Below this health it stops placing crystals right against itself. |
| `anchor` | `true` | Also use charged respawn anchors. |
| `trap` | `true` | Wall a low opponent in before detonating — one obsidian block per cycle, on a cardinal face, once they are under 40 % health. |
| `ender-pearls` | `true` | Pearl at the opponent's feet to close the gap, from 6 blocks out to 30. It also throws one the moment it sees a pearl leave the opponent's hand: crystal PvP is an aggressive format, the bot chases and it does not kite. |
| `break-blocks` | `true` | Real crystal PvP: the blasts destroy terrain and the obsidian the bot places stays where it put it. |

<Callout type="danger" title="break-blocks genuinely destroys the arena">
With `break-blocks: true` — the default — every crystal and anchor blast is a real explosion that
breaks terrain, and the obsidian the bot lays is real obsidian: not tracked, not aged out, not
tidied away. Over one fight the arena degrades exactly like a real one, and when the bot is removed
the craters and the obsidian are still there. That is the point of the mode, and it is also why you
do not run it in a world you care about. Turn it off and every block the bot places goes down
without a block update and is reverted afterwards, and the blasts stop breaking anything — the tidy
sandbox behaviour. The one thing that is cleaned up either way is a charged respawn anchor: it is
the only thing the bot can leave behind that kills somebody later, so it is always removed with the
bot.
</Callout>

## `MACE_PVP` — height is the damage

The mace is worth nothing on the ground and everything at the end of a fall, so the whole mode is
one loop: get height, stay over the target, land the smash. The damage scaling is vanilla, which
means height really is what makes the hit hurt — the bot is not being handed a big number.

- **The wind charge goes whenever the range is there.** It is cheap, it comes back in under two
  seconds, and a mace on the ground is a slow sword. The launch needs line of sight and 5.5 blocks
  or less.
- **The pearl is saved.** A pearl lift puts the bot twelve blocks up against the charge's seven, and
  costs most of a second of standing still before its feet leave the floor — so it waits for
  somebody already committed: eating, looking away, or under 45 % health.
- **It plants its feet first.** A short pause before the charge goes off under it. A launch nobody
  can read is not practice.
- **No two launches are the same height**, so the fall — and the damage at the end of it — varies
  the way a real one does.
- **A smash into a raised shield is a smash wasted**, so the axe goes first and the launch waits for
  the wind-up it costs.

`MACE_PVP` has no `advanced` block of its own: everything about it is the loop above, the global
`advanced.attack` and `advanced.movement`, and the difficulty.

## `BOXING` — hits, not damage

Nobody takes damage. Every hit still lands with its own knockback, its own invulnerability window
and its own sound — only the damage is gone, so a round is a hundred exchanges long instead of six
and a dropped combo costs a count rather than the round. The bot's own health bar is scenery too:
whatever you did to it is undone every tick, and fire is put out, so the round ends when somebody
walks away and not before.

<Callout type="warning" title="Boxing scores on the action bar, and deals no damage">
The count goes to the boxing player's action bar, from the `boxing-score` message in
`messages.yml`, with `%player_hits%` for the hits you landed and `%bot_hits%` for the hits it
landed on you:

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/messages.yml"
bot:
  boxing-score: '{secondary}Boxing {letters_black}▎ {letters}You {success}%player_hits% {letters_black}▎ {letters}Bot {error}%bot_hits%'
```

Eight seconds with no hit either way ends the round and the count starts again from zero. Nothing is
stored: the action bar is the whole scoreboard.
</Callout>

The bot spends the entire round pressuring — no healing, no spacing, no shield, no crit, because
none of them would do anything. It circles more than any mode but `SWORD`.

## A bot fights with the kit it is given

This is the limitation that surprises people. **A mode's tactics are gated on the bot actually
having the items for them**, and where those items come from depends on how the bot was spawned.

<Callout type="warning" title="A crystal bot with no crystals just swings its sword">
Spawn a bot through `/bot` and it has no kit: nothing is counted, and every style falls back to its
own figures — the obsidian, the crystals, the potions and the pearls never run out. That is what a
sandbox dummy is for.

Spawn one through the [API](/docs/exyliapracticebot/api) with a `BotSpec` carrying a `BotKit` — a
practice match handing the bot the same kit both sides fight — and the bot spends *that* kit and
nothing else. Every end crystal, obsidian block, respawn anchor, ender pearl, wind charge, splash
potion, golden apple, cobweb and bucket comes out of it and runs out. A kit with no end crystals is
a crystal bot that never starts a chain; a kit with no wind charges is a mace bot that never leaves
the ground; a kit with no axe cannot break your shield. What it does then is what is left: it swings
whatever weapon the kit gave it.
</Callout>

The kit also decides the gear, which is why the kit table above only applies to a bot dressed by its
mode. Given a kit, the bot wears it as it came — its enchantments, its durability, its names — and
works out for itself which item to hold: a mace over an axe over a sword over a trident, better tier
breaking a tie, judged by what wins a fight rather than by hotbar order. A shield or a totem in the
kit's off hand is read as a shield or a totem; anything else in that slot is worth nothing to the
AI. A kit with no weapon is a bot that punches, and that is not a reason to refuse a fight.

Pot PvP and UHC restock between rounds — seven seconds with no hit either way and the ledger goes
back to what the kit started as.

## Names and one-line summaries

What players read for each mode is `messages.yml`, not the enum:

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/messages.yml"
placeholders:
  mode:
    none: '{muted}Vanilla'
    sword: '{letters}Sword'
    pot-pvp: '{success}Pot PvP'
    uhc: '{warning}UHC'
    crystal-pvp: '{info}Crystal PvP'
    mace-pvp: '{error}Mace PvP'
    boxing: '{accent}Boxing'
    none-summary: 'Sword and shield. No consumables, no tricks.'
    sword-summary: 'Pure movement: strafes, w-taps, combos, almost no crits.'
    pot-pvp-summary: 'Crits, w-taps, combos, gapples and heal pots.'
    uhc-summary: 'Axe and shield: guard breaks, gapples, cobweb, lava and water.'
    crystal-pvp-summary: 'Obsidian, crystals, double taps, anchors and traps.'
    mace-pvp-summary: 'Wind charge launches into mace smashes.'
    boxing-summary: 'Nobody takes damage. Hits are counted instead.'
```

Changing a mode from the menu confirms with the `bot.mode-changed` message, which carries `%mode%`.

<Callout type="warning" title="The shipped mode-changed message overstates what happens">
Its default wording ends in "kit applied", and switching mode from the menu does not apply a kit —
only the settings' first build and `/bot reset` do. The menu item itself has it right ("Only the
techniques change. Your kit stays as you left it."), so until the default message is corrected,
reword `bot.mode-changed` to match:

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBot/messages.yml"
bot:
  mode-changed: '%prefix% {letters}Mode set to %mode% {letters_black}» {letters}techniques only.'
```
</Callout>

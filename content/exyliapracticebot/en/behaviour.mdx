description: "What a bot does when nobody is configuring it: when it fights, when it follows, the three ways it leaves on its own, and how it gets around terrain."
---

A bot is a mannequin with its own tick. Once it exists it thinks once per tick, on the entity's own
scheduler, and everything below happens without anybody touching a menu.

## Attack and Follow

Two switches decide what a bot does with its tick. Both are on by default, and both live in the
advanced menu.

| Switch | On | Off |
|---|---|---|
| **Attack** | It fights whoever it is pointed at. | It never engages and never swings. It still takes damage, still follows, still dies. |
| **Follow** | It closes the distance: to your heels out of combat, to fighting range in a fight. | It stands where it is. Out of combat it never walks to you; in a fight it holds its ground instead of chasing. |

Turning **Attack** off gives you a moving target that hits back at nothing. Turning **Follow** off
gives you a punching bag that stays exactly where you left it. Turning both off gives you a statue
that heals itself.

## When it decides to fight

A bot engages when all three of these are true:

- **Attack** is on.
- Its target is within the **engage distance**, `26` blocks.
- Its target is not in spectator mode.

Until then it is idle, and idle is not nothing:

- It keeps up with you when **Follow** is on and you are further away than the follow distance
  (`advanced.movement.follow-distance`, `3.0` blocks by default) plus a little slack, so it does not
  shuffle back and forth on the edge of the band.
- It lowers its shield, stops sneaking, and glances around every second or two.
- If nothing has hit it for five seconds and it is not eating, it heals back to full. A mannequin has
  no hunger and never regenerates on its own, so without this a practice bot would only ever get
  weaker.

<Callout type="tip" title="Twenty-six blocks is a walk">
Spawn a bot, walk away, and it will follow you without fighting. Walk back inside twenty-six blocks
and it starts. That is how you begin a rep on your terms instead of the bot's.
</Callout>

## When you hit it

The shield only blocks while it is genuinely raised. The AI lowers it to swing, so a player who
reads the rhythm gets a free hit — that is the whole point of shield timing existing.

| What you do | What happens |
|---|---|
| Hit a raised shield from the front | No damage. It still gets shoved, exactly like a blocking player. |
| Hit it with an axe | Its shield goes down for `advanced.defense.shield-disable-seconds`, `5.0` by default, with the break sound. |
| Blow it up | The blast is blocked only if the shield is pointed at where the blast went off. |
| Pop its totem | It takes a beat to get the next one into its hand, one at a time, like a player. How many it has is `advanced.health.totems`. |
| Kill it | Nothing drops and no experience falls. Its gear was issued by the server, not earned. |

If a bot got the last hit on you, your death message is hidden — the server does not announce a bot
kill in chat.

## The three ways a bot leaves on its own

Each has its own line in `messages.yml`, and each is measured against whoever the bot is currently
fighting rather than the player who paid for it.

| What happened | Message key | Default text |
|---|---|---|
| You died | `bot.removed-death` | "Bot removed: you died." |
| You changed worlds | `bot.removed-world-change` | "Bot removed: you changed worlds." |
| You went past the leash, `200` blocks | `bot.removed-too-far` | "Bot removed: you went too far away." |

Three more removals happen quietly, with no message, because there is nobody left to read one: the
target logs out or is kicked, the bot's mannequin stops being a valid entity, and the server shuts
down. A bot never survives a restart.

<Callout type="note" title="Why the target and not the owner">
A bot is measured against the player it is fighting. An owner who died and is now spectating from the
lobby may have left their bot mid-fight with somebody else, and taking it away there would end that
fight for a player who did nothing wrong.
</Callout>

## Respawning

A killed bot comes back on its own when `respawn.enabled` is on, which it is by default.

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBotV3/config.yml"
respawn:
  enabled: true
  delay-seconds: 2.0
```

You get `bot.respawning` — "Bot died! Respawning..." — immediately, and `bot.respawned` when it is
back. The respawn is cancelled if you log out in the meantime, and it is skipped if you are no longer
in one of the worlds listed under `worlds`. It also goes through the same spawn path as `/bot spawn`, so a
respawn cannot walk past a server cap that a fresh spawn would have been refused by. See
[Limits](/docs/exyliapracticebot/limits).

## Shift right-click

Sneak and right-click your own bot to open its menu. It is on by default and it is one key:

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaPracticeBotV3/config.yml"
interaction:
  shift-click-opens-menu: true
```

Only your own bot, only while sneaking. Right-clicking somebody else's bot does nothing, and neither
does a plain right-click on yours.

## How it moves

The bot steers straight at whatever it is walking to. That is the common case and it costs nothing:
a direction, a step up a single block, a jump. Terrain is what breaks it, and terrain is the only
thing the grid search under `advanced.pathfinding` exists for.

The search runs when direct steering has visibly failed — the bot has stopped making progress, or
there is a wall in front of it taller than one step — and it produces a short list of checkpoints to
walk through instead of the target itself. It is used only when the bot is closing in or following;
backing off and circling are never steered by a path towards the person it is backing away from.

| Key | Default | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| `enabled` | `true` | Off means the bot only ever steers straight at its target. It will walk into walls. |
| `max-search-radius-horizontal` | `16` | How far to the sides it may look. Also the range beyond which it does not bother searching at all. |
| `max-search-radius-vertical` | `10` | How far up and down it may look. |
| `max-expanded-nodes` | `200` | The size of one search. Raise it for arenas with real terrain, at a cost per bot per search. |
| `max-search-time-micros` | `1500` | The hard wall-clock ceiling on one search. It always returns inside this, with a partial route if that is all it found. |
| `max-drop-height-safe` | `3` | How far it will drop on the way. |
| `max-drop-height-protected` | `10` | The same, for a bot with Slow Falling or invincibility on — it cannot hurt itself, so it may take the shortcut. |
| `max-gap-jump-columns` | `1` | How wide a gap it will jump across. |
| `stuck-check-interval-ticks` | `10` | How often it asks whether it has moved. |
| `stuck-progress-epsilon` | `0.5` | How many blocks count as having moved. |
| `stuck-strikes-threshold` | `2` | How many checks in a row it has to fail before it counts as stuck. |
| `path-stale-distance` | `5.0` | How far the target may drift from where the route was aimed before the route is thrown away. |
| `path-max-age-ticks` | `60` | How old a route may get. Three seconds. |
| `checkpoint-reached-radius` | `0.6` | How close is close enough to a checkpoint. |
| `consecutive-failed-searches-before-cooldown` | `3` | How many searches that find nothing it takes to give up. |
| `give-up-cooldown-ticks` | `60` | How long it then walks straight at the target instead of searching. Three seconds. |

<Callout type="warning" title="Raise the budget before the radius">
`max-expanded-nodes` and `max-search-time-micros` are the two keys that cost tick time, and they cost
it once per bot per search. Widening the radius without widening the budget only means the search
runs out of budget further from home.
</Callout>

A bot also holds the chunk it is standing in open for as long as it exists. An entity only runs its
scheduled work while its chunk is ticking, and a bot in a chunk nobody is ticking never applies its
gear and never thinks: it stands there naked and still. Keeping its own chunk alive is what stops
that, and the ticket is handed back the moment the bot is removed.

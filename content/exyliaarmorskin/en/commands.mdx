## The player command

| Command | Permission | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `/wardrobe` | `exyliaarmorskin.wardrobe` | Opens the wardrobe. Alias: `/armorskins`. |

That is the only command a player needs. Skin **items** need no command at all: they are dragged onto
armour.

<Callout type="note" title="It refuses to open in item mode">
With `behaviour.mode: item` a wardrobe choice would never be read, so the command answers
`wardrobe-disabled` instead of opening a screen that changes nothing.
</Callout>

## The admin command

`/armorskinsadmin`, alias `/armorskinadmin`. Everything under it needs `exyliaarmorskin.admin`.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `give <player> <skin> [1-64]` | Gives the skin item for `<skin>`. |
| `remover <player> [1-64]` | Gives skin removers. |
| `list` | Lists every registered skin. |
| `refresh [player]` | Rebuilds and re-sends what a player is shown wearing. |
| `wardrobe open <player>` | Opens somebody's wardrobe for them. |
| `wardrobe clear <player>` | Empties their wardrobe: every slot back to their real armour. |
| `reload` | Re-reads this plugin's files and redraws everyone. |

## `give`

`<skin>` tab-completes from the registry, and an unknown id answers `unknown-skin` to whoever typed
the command. `[amount]` is 1 to 64 and defaults to 1.

`give` never checks the skin's own permission: it hands the item to anybody. Whether the player can
then **use** it is `exyliaarmorskin.skin.<id>` and the two switches in
[Permissions](/docs/exyliaarmorskin/permissions).

The item is added to the target's inventory; anything that does not fit is dropped at their feet. The
`given` message — with `%skin%` and `%amount%` — is sent **to the target**, not to the sender, so
giving from console tells the player and says nothing back.

## `remover`

The same shape without a skin id. The `given-remover` message, carrying `%amount%`, also goes to the
target.

## `list`

Prints `list-header`, then one `list-entry` per skin with `%id%`, `%skin%` and `%pieces%` filled in.
With nothing registered it prints `list-empty` instead. The list covers skins from `skins.yml` **and**
skins another plugin registered through the API.

## `refresh`

| Typed by | With a player | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| A player | no | Only that player is refreshed. |
| A player | yes | Only the named player is refreshed. |
| Console | no | **Everyone online** is refreshed. |
| Console | yes | Only the named player is refreshed. |

Refreshing re-reads the armour the player is actually wearing, rebuilds every visual from it and
re-sends both their inventory and their equipment to everyone tracking them. It is also what applies
a permission you just granted or revoked, because that is checked while the visual is rebuilt. It
plays no effects and touches no file — it is the answer to "another plugin changed this item behind
my back".

## `wardrobe open` and `wardrobe clear`

`open` shows the target their own wardrobe — the screen belongs to them, not to the admin who opened
it. `clear` empties every slot and answers `wardrobe-cleared`; the armour itself is untouched, since
a wardrobe was never written to it.

## `reload`

Runs one step, `configs`, which:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Re-reads every file">
    `config.yml`, `animations.yml`, `skins.yml` and `menus/wardrobe.yml`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-applies the message prefix">
    So `%prefix%` matches the rest of the ecosystem again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebuilds the skins">
    Cached trims are dropped, animations are rebuilt from `animations.yml`, and the file's skins
    replace the previous file's skins. Skins registered through the API are left alone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Redraws everyone online">
    Every player has their visuals rebuilt and re-sent, silently — no equip effects are played.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The step reports success or failure to whoever ran it. It does not reload ExyliaLib; `/exylialib
reload` is what does that.

<Callout type="note" title="Reload is safe mid-fight">
Nothing is removed from anybody: the armour keeps its id, the wardrobe keeps its rows, and the visual
is simply rebuilt. A player wearing an animated skin sees the new speed or colour on the next frame.
</Callout>

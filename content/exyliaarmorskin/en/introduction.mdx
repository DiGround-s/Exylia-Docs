---
title: "Introduction"
description: "What ExyliaArmorSkin is: a colour and a trim drawn over any armour piece with packets, while the real item keeps everything it had."
---

ExyliaArmorSkin puts a **skin** on a piece of armour. A skin is a colour, an optional trim and an
optional animation, and it is drawn on the way to the client: the netherite chestplate stays a
netherite chestplate on the server, and everyone sees blue leather with a vex trim.

The armour itself is never converted. Applying a skin writes **one value** into the item's data —
`exyliaarmorskin:skin`, the skin id — and nothing else changes. Enchantments, attributes, durability,
custom model data and any other plugin's data stay exactly where they were.

## What the client is shown

| On the real item | On the visual |
|---|---|
| Netherite chestplate | The leather piece of the same slot, in the skin's colour |
| Its trim, or none | The skin's trim, when the skin sets one |
| Its display name | The same name, or the vanilla name of the real material |
| Its lore | The same lore, then a blank line, then the `armor-lore` lines |
| Its enchantments | Hidden, but the glint is kept |
| Its durability | The real damage **and the real maximum**, so the bar reads true |
| Its attributes | Hidden, and never replaced by leather's |

## Who sees a skin

| Viewer | Sees |
|---|---|
| Everyone tracking the wearer | The skin, on the body |
| The wearer, in their own inventory and paper doll | The skin |
| The wearer **in creative** | The real armour — a creative client echoes its slots back to the server, so it must be shown the truth |
| Anyone looking at a skinned piece in a chest, hotbar or on the cursor | The real item with the skin's lore lines added |
| Everyone, when the **wearer** lacks that skin's permission | The real armour — the id stays on the item, or the row stays in the wardrobe, and the look returns with the node |
| Anyone looking at an **elytra** | The elytra. It sits in the chest slot but is not armour, and dressing it as a chestplate would cost the wearer their flight |

## What ships

Twenty skins in `skins.yml` — **every one of them animated** — and twenty-one animations in
`animations.yml`, built from seven animation types: `rainbow`, `pulse`, `frames`, `wave`, `sweep`,
`bloom` and `flicker`. Each skin also
carries a **set bonus**: particles around the body, a line when the set closes and another when it
breaks, once every piece it fits is worn with it.

Each skin owns a permission derived from its id, `exyliaarmorskin.skin.<id>`, with a node per slot
underneath it (`…skin.<id>.helmet`), and by default a player needs it both to wear the skin and to be
seen wearing it.

## Two ways a skin gets worn

| Way | Whose it is | Which server it suits |
|---|---|---|
| **A skin item** | The armour's: one value is written onto the piece and travels with it through chests, trades and deaths. | Survival, economy |
| **The wardrobe** | The player's: stored per player and per slot in a table, and it lands on whatever they equip. | Practice, PvP |

`behaviour.mode` decides which is read — `item`, `wardrobe` or `both` — and is covered in
[The wardrobe](/docs/exyliaarmorskin/wardrobe).

A skin item is used by dragging: drop it onto a piece of armour in your own inventory and it is
applied; drop a **remover** onto skinned armour and the skin comes off and is handed back as the item
again. The wardrobe is `/wardrobe`: one screen, click a slot, click a skin.

<Callout type="note" title="The armour is never rewritten">
An applied skin item writes a single value onto the piece; a wardrobe choice writes nothing at all.
Everything else is packets, so what any other plugin reads from that armour is exactly what it had.
</Callout>

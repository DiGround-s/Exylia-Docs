Writing a kill effect by hand means knowing twenty-six shapes, forty parameters and what `ease:in`
does to a falling sword. You do not have to. The prompt below carries all of it — the step language,
the limits, the three rules that save an afternoon, and two shipped effects in full — so the only
thing left for you is the idea.

## Write the idea first

The prompt is only as good as what you put in it. "Something cool" produces something generic; a
paragraph produces the effect you pictured. Say:

<Steps>
  <Step title="What it is made of">
    Real blocks and items, named. `NETHERITE_SWORD`, `AMETHYST_CLUSTER`, `CRYING_OBSIDIAN`. This is
    what gives an effect its silhouette — particles are only the light around it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What moves, and where">
    Falls from above, closes on the centre, throws itself outwards, rises and hangs. Give it a
    direction and a distance: "twelve blades fall from ten blocks up".
  </Step>
  <Step title="How long it lasts">
    A kill effect is a scene of a second and a half to three seconds. Say whether you want a single
    slam or three beats one after another.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What happens to the body">
    Every shipped effect choreographs the victim with a `[RAGDOLL]` line, frame by frame: the body
    dances, is slapped, spins or is spelled into a word, then bursts, collapses or dissolves. Say what
    it does — and whether a copy of the killer walks in to act it out, or the players watching join
    in. Or ask for no body at all.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The mood and the sound">
    Heavy and grinding, or bright and clean. Name a sound if you have one in mind; otherwise let the
    model choose and change it afterwards.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="A worked example of an idea">
"A funeral. The victim kneels, then falls forward. Eight `DEEPSLATE_TILES` pillars rise out of the
ground around them in a circle of three blocks, slowly, over a second. A `SOUL_LANTERN` hangs above
the body and glows. Muffled bell at the end. Cold, quiet, no fire."
</Callout>

## Copy the prompt

<AiPrompt id="killeffect" />

Write the idea in the box, copy the prompt with your idea already inside it, paste it into whichever
AI you use, and send it.

## Then

<Steps>
  <Step title="Paste the answer into effects.yml">
    Open `plugins/ExyliaKillEffect/effects.yml` and paste the block under `kill_effects:`, at the
    same indentation as the effects already there. Make sure its `category` is one that exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reload">
    `/killeffectsadmin reload`. Every player picking that effect is re-checked against the new
    catalogue, so an effect that stops existing is dropped from whoever had it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the console">
    A step that cannot be read is dropped and printed with the line that caused it. That is your
    proofreader: if nothing appears, every line was understood.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it, then tune it">
    Test it on yourself. Numbers are the easy part to change — `radius`, `life`, `size`, the
    `[DELAY]` between beats. The prompt asks the model to explain what each step does so you know
    which number to move.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="The model can still be wrong">
It writes a material that does not exist, or a parameter a shape does not understand, and the
console says so. It cannot break the plugin — a bad line is dropped, the rest of the effect still
plays — but it can waste an afternoon if you do not read the console. Give the error back to the AI
and it usually fixes it in one turn.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="It is a permission too">
The key you paste becomes `exyliakilleffect.effect.<key>`, so nobody sees the new effect until they
are granted it. See [Permissions](/docs/exyliakilleffect/permissions).
</Callout>

Everything the prompt knows is documented here too, if you would rather write the effect yourself:
[Sequences](/docs/exyliakilleffect/sequences) is the full step language, and
[Effects and categories](/docs/exyliakilleffect/effects) is the file around it.

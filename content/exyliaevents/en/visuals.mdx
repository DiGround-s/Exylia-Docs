## Sidebars

`scoreboards.yml` holds one sidebar per event type, plus a `lobby` board shown while players wait.
Each is an ExyliaLib sidebar: a title, a list of lines and a refresh interval in ticks.

```yaml title="scoreboards.yml"
scoreboards:
  lobby: ...
  tnttag: ...
  spleef: ...
  csgo: ...
  topkiller_teams: ...
```

Every registered type has its own key, team variants included, so a Quake board and a Quake Teams
board are edited separately.

The values available inside the lines are the ones that type provides — its round, its timer, its
score, the player's own kills — because each event builds its own scoreboard context.

<Callout type="note" title="TAB is not required for sidebars">
The sidebar is drawn by ExyliaLib either way. What TAB changes is the **handover**: when TAB is
installed, leaving an event restores the player to their normal TAB scoreboard and nametag rather
than to nothing.
</Callout>

## Boss bars

`config.yml` carries a boss bar per type that has one, in that type's own block:

```yaml title="config.yml"
tnttag:
  boss-bar:
    text: "{primary}Eliminating in {error}%time% ⌚"
    color: PURPLE
    style: NOTCHED_20
  tagged-action-bar:
    text: "..."
```

Types with their own visual blocks include TNT Tag, Tag Game, Block Party, Build Battle, Quake,
CS:GO, Parkour, Chairs, Slap, Gem Crawler, Pillars of Fortune, LMS, Simon Says, Survival Games and
Tournament, alongside `winner-fireworks` for the celebration at the end.

Tournament's block is three separate effects rather than a bar: a freeze title before a match, the
same countdown on the action bar, and an "you're next" title for whoever fights after.

## Nametags

With TAB installed, nametags are handed to TAB when the event ends. Without it, nothing is changed
and nothing needs restoring.

## Visibility

Two managers decide who sees whom: spectators are hidden from players inside the game, and players in
one event are hidden from players in another running at the same time. That is what stops two
simultaneous events in nearby arenas from showing each other's players.

## Music

Events can play music discs to their participants:

```yaml title="config.yml"
settings:
  event-music-enabled: true
  event-music-volume: 0.5
  event-music-interval: 150
  event-music-discs: [MUSIC_DISC_PIGSTEP, MUSIC_DISC_CAT, ...]
```

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `event-music-enabled` | `true` | Whether events play music at all. |
| `event-music-volume` | `0.5` | How loud. |
| `event-music-interval` | `150` | Seconds between discs. |
| `event-music-discs` | 19 discs | The pool it picks from. |

A player who does not want it uses `/events music`, which is stored per player and remembered.

<Callout type="tip" title="Turning it off server-wide">
`event-music-enabled: false` silences it for everyone and makes the per-player toggle moot — the
usual choice on a server that already has its own ambience.
</Callout>

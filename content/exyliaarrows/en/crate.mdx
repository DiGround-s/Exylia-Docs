---
title: "Rarities and the crate"
description: "The rarities effects are sorted into, the keys that open a crate, and what a player keeps after it lands."
---

An effect is normally sold as a permission node. The crate is the other way in: a rarity decides how
likely it is, a key buys one roll, and what comes out is written onto the player's own row rather
than granted as a node — so it outlives the rank that never granted it.

## The rarities

`config.yml`'s `tiers` block declares them. Nothing about the four below is hard-coded: rename them,
delete one, invent `mythic`.

```yaml
tiers:
  common:
    name: "Common"
    material: GRAY_DYE
    color: "{muted}"
    chance: 60.0
    priority: 1
  rare:
    name: "Rare"
    material: LIGHT_BLUE_DYE
    color: "{info}"
    chance: 25.0
    priority: 2
  epic:
    name: "Epic"
    material: PINK_DYE
    color: "{accent}"
    chance: 12.0
    priority: 3
  legendary:
    name: "Legendary"
    material: ORANGE_DYE
    color: "{highlight}"
    chance: 3.0
    priority: 4
```

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `name` | What menus and placeholders call it. |
| `material` | The item the rarity is drawn with. |
| `color` | The colour its name and its effects are written in. |
| `chance` | How often a crate lands on it, **relative to the other rarities**. |
| `priority` | Where it sits in menus; lower comes first. |

An effect names its rarity with `tier` in `effects.yml`, and omitting it makes the effect common:

```yaml title="effects.yml"
INFERNO:
  category: ember
  tier: epic
```

<Callout type="note" title="chance is a weight, not a percentage">
The shares are worked out from the weights every time they are needed, so deleting a rarity
re-spreads its odds instead of leaving a gap, and weights that sum to seven are as honest as weights
that sum to a hundred. A rarity nothing declares an effect for is left out of the roll entirely.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A rarity that is gone costs its effects their colour, never their existence">
An effect naming a `tier` the file no longer declares falls back to the first rarity — the cheapest
one on a sane file. Renaming `legendary` to `mythic` demotes every legendary rather than deleting it.
</Callout>

## Keys

Two kinds, and the crate takes both. A number on the player's row is impossible to drop, dupe or
lose to a full inventory; an item can be sold at a stall, put in a kit and given to a friend.

### On the row

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/arrowsadmin keys give <player> <amount>` | Adds keys. Alias: `keys add`. |
| `/arrowsadmin keys take <player> <amount>` | Takes them away. Never below zero. Alias: `keys remove`. |
| `/arrowsadmin keys set <player> <amount>` | Sets the number outright. |
| `/arrowsadmin keys check <player>` | Prints what they hold. |

Every one of them runs from console, which is how a shop, a vote listener, a crate key from another
plugin or a rank reward hands keys out:

```text
/arrowsadmin keys give %player% 1
```

`crate.start-keys` is what a player is given the first time they are seen — written once, when the
row is created, never again on later joins.

### In the hand

```text
/arrowsadmin item key <player> [1-64]
```

Right click a bound crate block holding one and it opens on the spot: one key, one reel, no question
screen. The key leaves the hand as the reel starts falling. A left click never spends one, and
neither does a right click anywhere else — a key cannot be placed as a block, so it cannot be lost to
a wall. How it is drawn is `key-item` in `config.yml`, and it is told apart by what it carries in its
data rather than by its name or material.

## Opening it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the crate screen">
    `/arrows crate`, the **EFFECT CRATE** button in the effects menu, or a crate block in the world.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick how many at once">
    One key opens one crate. `crate.max-at-once` decides how many choices are offered, capped at
    seven by the width of the opening screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it fall">
    One reel per crate, each landing `crate.stagger-seconds` after the one before it. What a reel
    came within one slot of stays on screen, because half of what a crate is worth is the legendary
    that went past the line.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The roll itself is two rolls: a rarity by its weight, then an effect inside it with every effect
equally likely. That is what makes the odds in the menu true — a legendary at three percent stays at
three percent whether the file declares two legendaries or forty.

<Callout type="note" title="Paid out as each reel lands">
The keys are spent and the prizes written down before the first frame, but nothing is handed over
until a reel stops on the line. Four crates opened at once pay out one at a time, `stagger-seconds`
apart, each with its own sound and its own line in chat. Closing the window costs nothing — the reels
stop being drawn and the prizes still land on their own clock. Leaving the server ends the timer, so a reel
still in the air is settled on the way out: unlocked on the row now, and any token kept in the reward
queue until their next join.
</Callout>

## What a win is worth

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| Something new | Whatever `crate.reward` says: `UNLOCK` writes the effect onto the player's row, owned for good; `ITEM` hands over only its token, to be bound to a bow, sold or given away; `BOTH` does both. |
| Something already owned | `crate.duplicate-refund` keys are handed back instead. Under `ITEM` nothing is unlocked, so no opening is ever a duplicate and every key pays out. |
| No keys | The crate says so and spends nothing. |
| Nothing to hand over | A crate with no rarities, or none with effects in them, refuses rather than charging for the disappointment. |

An effect is usable when **either** its permission node applies **or** the player won it. That is the
whole difference between the two ways in: a node is a rank and stops when the rank does, a win is
theirs.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/arrowsadmin unlocks grant <player> <effect>` | Marks an effect as won, without a crate. Alias: `unlocks give`. |
| `/arrowsadmin unlocks revoke <player> <effect>` | Takes a won effect back, for a support ticket. Alias: `unlocks take`. |
| `/arrowsadmin unlocks clear <player>` | Wipes what they won, for a new season. |

## Crate blocks

A block in the world that opens the crate when it is clicked: a barrel in the hub, an ender chest in
every world, the statue somebody built.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/arrowsadmin crate block add` | Binds the block you are looking at. |
| `/arrowsadmin crate block remove` | Unbinds it. |
| `/arrowsadmin crate block list` | Prints every bound block. |
| `/arrowsadmin crate block clear` | Unbinds all of them. |

Bind them in game rather than by hand: the command writes the block you are looking at into
`crate.blocks`, and a bound block cannot be broken, blown up or pushed away while it is on that
list. A crate drawn as a barrel does not open a barrel either.

The list lives in `config.yml`, not in the world, so the blocks survive a world edit, a schematic
paste and a rollback — and a server that moves its hub only edits the list.

## The two screens

| File | What it draws |
|---|---|
| `menus/crate.yml` | The crate: how many keys the player holds, how much of the catalogue they have unboxed, and the one question of how many to open at once. |
| `menus/crate_open.yml` | The crates opening: one reel per crate, falling down its own column, with the prize landing on the third row. |

Both are ordinary Exylia menu files — slots, templates, sounds and actions. `crate.spin-frames`
decides how long the first reel falls (capped at 200, about eleven seconds), and `crate.on-spin`,
`crate.on-win` and `crate.on-duplicate` are full effect blocks: sound, title, action bar, boss bar,
particle and firework. They play as each reel lands rather than once at the end, so four crates
opened together read as four prizes, each with its own sound and its own line in chat.

---
title: "Inventory restore"
description: "A copy of what every player was carrying, kept for the days a staff member needs to hand it back."
---

Inventory restore keeps a copy of what a player was carrying, so a staff member reading a report
three days later can look at what they actually had and give it back. Copies are rows in the
database, not files, so the whole network reads the same history.

Module id `restore`, switch `modules.restore`, files under `modules/restore/`.

## Commands

| Command | Permission | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `/invrestore <player>` | `exyliastaff.restore` | Opens that player's saved inventories. `/inventoryrestore` and `/restoreinv` are aliases. |

That is the whole command surface. Everything else — saving a copy now, deleting one, purging a
player, reading the history — is a button in the menus, so there is one thing to remember and one
place to look.

The argument is a network name: everybody connected to the proxy is suggested, not only the players
this server has seen. A name this server has never met is asked of the proxy through the staff
member who typed it, which is why console cannot reach one. The bare command answers with its own
usage lines rather than the server's parser error.

Two more permissions gate buttons rather than commands:

| Permission | What it opens |
|---|---|
| `exyliastaff.restore.log` | The restore history, from either end. |
| `exyliastaff.restore.purge` | Dropping every backup of one player. |

## When a copy is taken

| Cause | Icon | Switch |
|---|---|---|
| Death | `SKELETON_SKULL` | `causes.death` |
| Quit | `IRON_DOOR` | `causes.quit` |
| Join | `OAK_DOOR` | `causes.join` |
| World change | `ENDER_PEARL` | `causes.world-change` |
| Manual save | `WRITABLE_BOOK` | Always on |
| Before a restore | `CLOCK` | Always on |

The last two have no switch on purpose. A manual save is a staff member asking for it, and the copy
taken before a restore is what makes a restore undoable.

A copy is only written when there is something to copy. `min-seconds-between` (60) is the gap
between two ordinary backups of the same player, so somebody bouncing through a portal does not
write a row a second — deaths and manual saves ignore it, because those are the ones staff come
looking for. Two backups minutes apart holding the same items are still two backups: the one a staff
member wants is the one at the moment they are asking about.

The quit copy is skipped while the server is stopping. Paper disconnects every player before it
disables a plugin, so a quit backup there is one row per online player written in the same second,
and the join copy says the same thing.

## How long they are kept

| Key | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `keep-per-player` | `20` | Backups kept per player. The oldest are dropped as new ones arrive. |
| `keep-days` | `14` | Days a backup is kept. `0` keeps them until the count drops them. |
| `history-keep-days` | `90` | Days the restore history is kept. `0` keeps it forever. |

Deaths and manual saves are not pushed out by the count. Those are what a report is about, so they
stay until `keep-days` takes them rather than being shoved aside by ordinary joins. The purge runs
when the module starts and once an hour after that.

## The screens

**Servers** opens first: which server each backup was taken on, the current one first and glowing.
**Inventories** lists that player's backups newest first, tabbed by event, each drawn with the icon
of its cause and a line saying what the event was. **Backup** is one of them, laid out with its own
armour and off-hand zones — the preview stacks are buttons, and their lore says so. Empty slots are
drawn as air rather than filler panes, so what you see is what they had.

**Restore** is where you tick the parts, and **Restore history** is the record of who handed what to
whom, readable from either end: what a staff member gave out, and what a player received.

## What you can hand back

Eleven parts, ticked one by one before you confirm:

`INVENTORY`, `ARMOR`, `OFF_HAND`, `ENDER_CHEST`, `EXPERIENCE`, `HEALTH`, `HUNGER`,
`POTION_EFFECTS`, `GAME_MODE`, `FLIGHT`, `LOCATION`.

`default-parts` is what is already ticked when the screen opens; the default is inventory, armour,
off hand, experience, health and hunger. A name this server does not know is skipped rather than
throwing, and a list that leaves nothing at all falls back to those six: a typo must not open a
screen where every box is empty.

The four parts made of items — inventory, armour, off hand and ender chest — can be *added* to what
the player is carrying instead of written over it. `default-replace` (`true`) decides which of the
two the screen opens on. Replacing saves their current items first either way, so the wrong choice
is one restore away from being undone.

`LOCATION` is the one part this module puts back itself rather than the library, because the
coordinates live on the backup row. It is never applied across servers: a location taken somewhere
else is not a place on this one.

`notify-target` (`true`) tells the player when a staff member hands their items back.

## Players who are offline

A hand-back for somebody who is not connected is queued with its own copy of the items and of the
parts you ticked, and lands the moment they join — their arrival backup is written first, so the two
never blend into one row. If they are online on another server, that server is nudged over Redis so
the delivery happens at once instead of waiting for a relog. A staff member who queued one by
mistake can drop what is still owed.

Four of the types in the catalogue are colours, and the only thing separating them is what they paint.

| Type | File | Paints |
|---|---|---|
| `nick_color` | `nick-colors.yml` | the player's name |
| `chat_color` | `chat-colors.yml` | the message they typed |
| `shadow_color` | `shadow-colors.yml` | what is drawn under that message |
| `rank_color` | `rank-colors.yml` | the prefix and suffix their rank already writes |

All four read the same [colour grammar](/docs/exyliachatcosmetics/catalogue) — shadows with one
addition of their own — and all four share the fields every catalogue entry has.

## The shape of an entry

Nick, chat and rank colours read exactly two keys of their own: the colour, and an animation.

```yaml
nick_colors:
  nebula:
    category: premium
    name: '{primary}&lNEBULA'
    gradient: ['#8a51c4', '#ff6b9d', '#59a4ff']   # or color: '#8a51c4'
    animation: shine
    icon: ENDER_EYE
    description: 'Three colours, and none of them sit still.'
    priority: 1
```

`color:` takes one colour as a string; `gradient:` takes a list of them instead. Write one or the
other — an entry with neither has nothing to paint with, and is reported and skipped. `animation:`
names an entry from [`animations.yml`](/docs/exyliachatcosmetics/animations) and is optional; with one,
the colour becomes the palette the animation moves through.

A palette token works as a stop like any other colour, so `gradient: ['{primary}', '{secondary_light}',
'{letters}']` is a gradient that follows your server rather than a fixed one. That is exactly what the
shipped EXYLIA entries do.

## Nick colours

`cosmetics/nick-colors.yml`, section `nick_colors`. **90 colours in six tabs.**

| Tab | Count |
|---|---|
| RED, BLUE, YELLOW, GREEN | 15 each, one flat shade per row |
| ADVANCED | 15 gradients |
| PREMIUM | 15 gradients that move |

The tabs are the chat colour tabs, and an id here is the id there: wearing CRIMSON on both paints the
name and the message in the same red, and the line reads as one decision rather than two.

A name is four to sixteen letters, which is why nothing here is darker than a name can afford to be —
the deepest shades live in the chat colours, where there is room for them. For the same reason a
gradient across a name wants two stops, three at the most: two read as a gradient, three read as a
flourish, and more than that reads as noise on eight characters.

## Chat colours

`cosmetics/chat-colors.yml`, section `chat_colors`. **96 colours in six tabs.**

| Tab | Count |
|---|---|
| RED, BLUE, YELLOW, GREEN | 16 each, solid and readable on the vanilla chat background |
| ADVANCED | 16 gradients |
| PREMIUM | 16 gradients that move |

A message from somebody wearing no chat colour falls back to `chat.default-color` in `config.yml`,
which reads the same grammar — a hex, a name, a `{token}` or a gradient. Left empty, the message is
not touched at all and arrives in whatever colour the format gave it.

## Shadow colours

`cosmetics/shadow-colors.yml`, section `shadow_colors`. **90 colours in six tabs.**

The shadow is the darker copy of the text the client draws under every line. A shadow colour replaces
it for that player's messages, and only for those.

| `color` | Means |
|---|---|
| `#rrggbb` | one colour under the whole line |
| `#rrggbbaa` | the same, the last two digits saying how strong |
| `{primary}` and any palette token | that colour, opaque |
| `auto` | the message's own colour, darkened to a quarter |
| `auto:0.5` | the same, saying by how much — `0` to `1` |
| `none` | no shadow at all, not even the client's own |

There is no `gradient:` here. A shadow is one colour under a whole line, or the line's own colour
darkened; a shadow that changed colour halfway would be a second message rather than a shadow.

| Tab | Count | Built for |
|---|---|---|
| NATURAL | 15 | `auto` at every strength — the shadow follows whatever the message is |
| RED, BLUE, YELLOW, GREEN | 15 each | sitting under the chat colour tab of the same name |
| PREMIUM | 15 | named after the PREMIUM chat colours, to be worn as a pair |

Alpha is how strong the shadow is: `aa` reads as depth, `55` as a glow, and no alpha at all as a hard
block of colour. NATURAL is what the ADVANCED and PREMIUM gradients want, because a gradient should
cast a gradient.

<Callout type="note" title="Nothing ships as `none`">
Taking the shadow off is what the browser's own "take it off" click already does, and a cosmetic that
does nothing is a row a player has to read to learn that it does nothing. The value still works if you
want it as a row of your own.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="1.21.4 and newer">
Shadow colours are drawn by clients on 1.21.4 and newer. An older one sees the message exactly as it
always did — nothing fails, and nothing else about the line changes.
</Callout>

## Rank colours

`cosmetics/rank-colors.yml`, section `rank_colors`. **77 colours in six tabs.** Needs LuckPerms.

A rank colour repaints the prefix the permission plugin gives the player, and the suffix with it. It
never touches the name — that is what nick colours are for. What the rank wrote keeps its shape, its
bold and its spacing; only its colour is replaced. Wearing none leaves the prefix and suffix exactly
as the rank wrote them.

### `repaint`

By default only letters and digits are repainted, so a rank's own frame survives:

| `rank-colors.repaint` in `config.yml` | `&8[&eVIP&8]` painted `#55ffff` |
|---|---|
| `letters` (default) | `&8[&bVIP&8]` — the grey brackets stay grey |
| `all` | `&b[VIP]` — everything the rank wrote, brackets included |

A gradient runs over the characters it may paint and not over the brackets it never reaches, and an
animation moves over exactly the same characters — an animation that reached the brackets would undo
the one thing `letters` is for. A prefix written with no colours of its own is painted whole either
way.

### Who may wear one

`permission` defaults to **`false`** for this type, the other way round from every other one, because
a rank colour is more often something a rank owns than something a player buys. That is only the
default an entry falls back to when it writes no `permission` of its own.

| Entry | Who may wear it |
|---|---|
| no `groups`, no `permission` | everybody |
| `groups: [owner, admin]` | whoever is in one of those LuckPerms groups, case-insensitively |
| `permission: true` | whoever has `exyliachatcosmetics.rank_color.<id>`, plus grants |

The shipped file uses all three. Its 15 SOLID entries write neither key and are free to every rank;
the 54 entries of METAL, GEMS, GRADIENT and ANIMATED each write `permission: true` and are bought with
a node or a grant; the 8 STAFF entries write `groups:` and are owned by being in one of them.

```yaml
rank_colors:
  azure:
    category: solid
    name: '{primary}&lAZURE'
    color: '{info}'
    priority: 2
  owner:
    category: staff
    name: '{primary}&lOWNER'
    gradient: ['#ffd700', '#ff8c00']
    animation: shine
    groups: [owner]
    icon: NETHER_STAR
    priority: 1
```

### The tabs

| Tab | Count | Meant for |
|---|---|---|
| SOLID | 15 | free to every rank — one flat colour, always legible |
| METAL | 12 | bronze through obsidian, by permission |
| GEMS | 15 | the cut stones, by permission |
| GRADIENT | 15 | two or three stops across the prefix, by permission |
| ANIMATED | 12 | the same, moving, by permission |
| STAFF | 8 | named by `groups:` rather than by a node |

<Callout type="warning" title="The STAFF tab names guesses at your groups">
`owner`, `admin`, `dev`, `mod`, `helper`, `builder`, `media`, `veteran` — change them to whatever
LuckPerms actually calls the groups on this server, or the tab is empty for everybody.
</Callout>

A prefix is short: two stops read as a gradient across it, three read as a flourish, and a prefix of
four letters cannot show more than that.

## Which colour wins

Players can also mix colours of their own, and those are separate types
([custom cosmetics](/docs/exyliachatcosmetics/custom)). Where two could apply, the more deliberate
choice wins — the one somebody mixed themselves.

| What is painted | First one wins |
|---|---|
| the name | `customnick`, then `nick_color`, then nothing |
| the prefix and suffix | `customrank`, then `rank_color`, then the rank as it wrote itself |
| the message | `customcolor`, then `chat_color`, then `chat.default-color` |
| under the message | the worn `shadow_color`, else whatever the server configured |

A rank colour has no say over the name: it paints what the rank owns, and the name belongs to the
player. A nick colour has no say over the prefix, for the same reason read the other way.

Where a shadow colour is worn, the server's own shadow is cleared from the line first and the player's
is drawn instead. Otherwise the line keeps whatever `text-shadow` ExyliaLib was configured with.
